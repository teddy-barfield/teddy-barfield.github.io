The subway comeback

The question

In March 2020 New York's transit ridership collapsed. How much of it has come back, and has it come back equally for the subway, buses and commuter rail, on weekdays and on weekends? Every figure below is computed in your browser from the processed data files, so none is typed in by hand.

One thing to know first: ridership is shown as a share of a baseline, and the baseline is the first week of March 2020 (), the last normal week in this dataset. It is not the MTA's own comparison with 2019. Details are under "How to read this".

    Recovery curves by mode

    Each line is the average daily ridership in a month, as a share of that mode's baseline. 100% means the same as the baseline week. Click a name in the legend to show or hide a line; the table underneath lists the same modes with their latest figures.

    A line chart fits a quantity followed over time, and putting the modes on one share-of-baseline scale lets a small system and a huge one be compared fairly. The most recent month is partial.

    Weekday versus weekend

    Pick a mode to compare its weekday and weekend recovery.

    Take the weekend line with care. The baseline for each weekend day is a single day (one Saturday and one Sunday in early March 2020), so a share above 100% can partly reflect an unusually quiet baseline day, not only strong weekend travel. The weekday baseline averages five days and is steadier.

    The biggest days

    The ten days with the most riders for a mode since January 1, 2021. Days are ranked by the raw count of riders, not by share of baseline, because the weekend baselines are single days and would put weekend days on top. Days before 2021 are left out because the busiest days in the whole dataset are simply the baseline week and the days just before the shutdown.

    How to read this, and what it leaves out

    The MTA's published daily counts are estimates of trips: subway and bus counts come from fare-payment data (swipes and taps), the railroads from ticket and onboard counts, and Access-A-Ride (AAR) is trips by the paratransit service. They count trips, not people, and the methods can change over time. Bridges and Tunnels (BT) counts vehicle crossings, not riders. Modes with no baseline (the central business district congestion-pricing entries, which only begin in 2025) are not shown.

    Some single days look like data glitches, such as a bus count near zero in May 2020, and a few days are missing for some modes. Rows with a missing or zero count were dropped. A write-up of what the comeback means, with fuller caveats, is planned as the next step.
