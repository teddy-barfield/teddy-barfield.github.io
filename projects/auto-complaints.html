The question

New York's Department of Financial Services (DFS) ranks every auto insurer each year by its complaint ratio: the number of consumer complaints DFS upheld against the insurer, per $1 million of premium the insurer wrote in New York. Dividing by premium puts a small company and a very large one on the same scale. This page shows where each insurer stands, how the picture changed from 2009 to 2024, and which insurers stand out.

          Approach and caveats

          The ratio is DFS's own published figure; this page does not recompute it. It counts only complaints DFS upheld, not every complaint filed, and the denominator is premium rather than the number of policies. Insurers with very little premium can show huge ratios on one or two complaints, so the best and most improved lists require a minimum size, stated beside each list.

          The source's 2016 table is identical to its 2015 table in all 158 rows, which looks like a publishing repeat, so 2016 is omitted from the chart. Year-to-year changes for an individual insurer can reflect a handful of complaints. A full methodology write-up is planned as the next step in this project.
