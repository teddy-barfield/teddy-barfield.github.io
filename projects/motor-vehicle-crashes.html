The question

Where and when do crashes happen in New York, and where and when are they more serious? An actuary separates frequency (how often crashes happen) from severity (how bad they are when they do), so this page shows both. Every figure is computed in your browser from the processed data files.

    Crashes per resident, by county

    Do not read this as a driving-risk ranking.
    CountyCrashes per 1,000 residents per yearFatal crashes per 100,000 per yearInjury or fatal shareGroup

    When crashes happen: hour by day of week

    Each cell is one hour of one weekday, summed over four years. Darker means more (or, in the second view, more serious). Read down a column to follow a day, across a row to compare days at the same hour.

    Weather, road surface and light

    ConditionCrashesShare of all crashesFatal per 1,000 crashesInjury or fatal share

    Conditions describe the crash, not the risk of driving in them: far more people drive on dry roads in clear weather, and no exposure (miles driven in each condition) is available here. The "Unknown" rows have almost no fatal crashes, which suggests those fields are left blank mostly on minor crashes, so treat them as a data-quality gap.

    How to read this, and what it leaves out

    The fuller methodology and caveats (reporting thresholds, exposure) are planned as the next step of this project. In short: counts are crash cases, "injury or fatal" combines the Fatal, Injury and Property Damage & Injury levels (my grouping), crashes per resident is not crashes per mile driven, and hour 0 (midnight) holds roughly double the crashes of hour 1, which probably reflects a default time for unknown times.
