About the data

  • Source: NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene, New York City Leading Causes of Death, via NYC Open Data. Rows are the leading causes within each year, sex and race/ethnicity group; deaths outside a group's leading causes fall under "All other causes".
  • Cause names were standardized across years (for example, spelling variants and revised ICD code lists were merged). Sex labels and race/ethnicity labels were unified.
  • Small counts are suppressed in the source to protect privacy. When a single row combines a year, sex, race/ethnicity group and cause, a count of only one or two deaths could make it possible to identify the people who died. Counts that small also give unreliable rates, which swing widely from year to year. In this file, suppression affects only the "Other" and "Not stated" groups in 2007–2014; from 2015 on, even counts of 1 are published. At most this leaves out a few hundred deaths in total, under 0.1%, but filtering to those two groups in those years can show zero where the true count was small but not zero.
  • Share is each cause's percentage of all deaths (every cause) in the same years and selected groups, so it shows how the mix of causes shifts even when total deaths change. With Compare by set to sex or race, Share instead shows how deaths split between those groups each year, and how each cause's deaths split between them. With Compare by set to Total, it shows the selected causes' combined share of all deaths.
  • Rates are per 100,000 people. The source gives rates only for Asian & Pacific Islander, Black, Hispanic and White groups, so rates leave out "Other" and "Not stated". Combined crude rates are rebuilt from the population implied by each group's deaths and rate. Combined age-adjusted rates are population-weighted averages of the group rates, a close approximation rather than an official figure.
  • 2020 and 2021 include COVID-19 as a separate cause, which accounts for most of the jump in those years.
