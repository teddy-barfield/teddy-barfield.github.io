How the numbers are calculated

Mortality

Death rates follow Makeham's law, where the force of mortality at age x is μx = A + B·cx. Parameters were fitted to approximate recent US population period life tables:

Male0.000607.50 × 10−51.0838
Female0.000303.25 × 10−51.0923

The health class multiplies the force of mortality. When underwriting is switched on, a select factor of min(1, 0.45 + 0.055·k) is also applied in policy year k, so the benefit of passing a medical exam fades out over ten years. One-year death probabilities are qx = 1 − exp(−∫xx+1 μt dt).

Timing

The model runs month by month. Deaths are spread evenly through each year of age (the uniform distribution of deaths assumption), premiums are paid at the start of each month or year while the insured is alive, and the death benefit is paid at the end of the month of death.

Expected values

With v = 1/(1+i) and death benefit DB, the expected present value of benefits is the monthly term insurance A1(12)x:n̅| scaled by the benefit:

EPV(benefits) = DB · Σm=012n−1 v(m+1)/12 · m/12px · 1/12qx+m/12

The expected present value of premiums is a temporary life annuity-due on the payment schedule:

EPV(premiums) = Σpayment times t P · vt · tpx

The actuarially fair (net) premium sets the two equal. Real premiums sit above or below it depending on the insurer's mortality assumptions, expenses, commissions, profit margin and the lapse rate it expects.

Whole life

Whole life coverage runs to age 120, where the model treats death as certain, so EPV(benefits) becomes the whole life insurance A(12)x. Premiums run for life or for the limited period chosen.

Not included

Cash values, dividends and policy loans on whole life policies, policy lapses, taxes, riders, conversion privileges, return-of-premium features, insurer expenses and the risk of insurer default. This tool is for education and is not financial advice.
